Small and quiet: a strip, a square only for the code

The strip is the whole story while watching, confirming and paid: logo and one or two short words, no place or customer name. It is drawn small (default 124 × 44 pt, under 3:1) so the window never competes with Square. Only a sale grows it into a 200 pt square, and it shrinks back after Paid. Till chooses the shape; iOS and the cashier choose the size, and the content scales with it. Corners stay empty for iOS's ✕ and ⤢.

Sharp, not blurry. Frames are drawn at the window's real pixel size. iOS tells Till its render size; Till draws exactly that many pixels (points × the screen's scale, 2× or 3×, never a smaller frame stretched up), keeps the QR on whole pixels, and redraws when the size changes. Text and the code stay crisp at every size.

Below about 200 pt a phone camera can't read the code reliably, so the small window shows the amount and a prompt instead. Confirming and Paid keep to the strip with a word and a glyph: colour is never the only signal.
